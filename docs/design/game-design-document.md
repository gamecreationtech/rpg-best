# Falling Sky: Game Design Document

Status: foundation decided on 2026-09-27. Sections marked TBD are still open.

## 1. Overview

- **Working title:** Falling Sky
- **Genre:** Isometric action role-playing game in the tradition of Diablo 2
- **Platforms:** Web browser on phones, tablets and desktop. Installable as a
  Progressive Web App (home-screen icon, full screen, offline capable).
- **Engine:** Custom game layer on Three.js, written in TypeScript
- **Target audience:** Diablo 2 veterans and action RPG players who want a real
  loot game on their phone with no download
- **One-line pitch:** A grim gothic Diablo 2 style loot game that runs in any
  browser, looks far better than its download size suggests, and stays smooth
  with two hundred monsters on screen.

## 2. Core pillars

Every feature must serve at least one of these. If it does not, cut it.

1. **The loot hunt.** Killing monsters drops gear with random properties. Finding,
   comparing and equipping items is the heartbeat of the game.
2. **Crowd combat that stays smooth.** Big packs of monsters and big spells, at a
   steady 60 frames per second on a mid-range phone. Performance is a feature.
3. **Grim beauty from pure code.** Every sprite, tile, animation and sound is
   generated by code. The look is isometric pixel art in the spirit of
   Diablo 1 and 2, sold by torchlight that fades into dithered darkness.
   Nothing may look like a placeholder.
4. **One thumb is enough.** Tap to move, tap to attack, tap a skill. The whole
   game is playable on a phone held in one hand.

## 4. Gameplay

### 4.1 Core loop

- **Minute to minute:** Tap to move through a dark procedurally generated area,
  fight packs of monsters with spells, pick up drops, manage health and mana.
- **Session (10 to 30 minutes):** Clear a dungeon level or two, portal back to
  town, sell junk, equip upgrades, spend skill points, go deeper.
- **Long term:** Push through acts, hunt rare and unique items, build the hero
  toward a personal playstyle through the skill trees.

### 4.2 Heroes and progression

- **Three classes:** Knight (sword and shield, plate), Sorcerer (staff,
  elemental spells) and Rogue (bow or daggers, traps and stealth).
- **Pledges:** at level 20 each class swears one of three pledges, which
  unlocks its own skills: Paladin, Titan, Nightlord; Necromancer, Stormsinger,
  Wintercaller; Quiverbound, Impaler, Silverblade. The choice is a full-screen
  moment: the hero is held in place and cannot be hurt until it is made.
- **Skills:** base skills per class, pledge skills, and ultimates that replace
  a rank-5 base skill using the ultimate point earned at level 20. One skill
  point, one passive point and five attribute points per level. The level cap
  is 100.
- **Passives:** a general tree plus one tree per class, with prerequisites.
- **Stats:** Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Vitality. Full formulas are in
  `docs/design/systems-reference.md` and the export they came from.

### 4.2b Zones (first draft)

Eleven zones reached from the town waypoint, each with its own map
generator, colours and monsters, and a level that its monsters scale to: the
Proving Grounds (open field, level 1), the Cursed Hollow (caves, 6), the Ashen
Marsh (sunken ruins, 12), the Frozen Crypt (halls and corridors, 18), the
Ember Foundry (26), the Sunken Temple (35), the Blighted Orchard (45), the
Obsidian Halls (58), the Storm Peaks (72), the Abyss (88) and the Throne of
the Fallen (100). Drafted by the engineer until the producer's zone and
monster data replaces them.

### 4.2c Leveling pace

The level cap is 100. A level is meant to take about a minute and a half of
fighting at the start and about twenty at the end, twenty hours in all,
in a zone of the hero's own level. The experience needed is derived from
that target and the measured rate at each level, so the pace stays put when
monster numbers change; the numbers live in `docs/design/systems-reference.md`.

### 4.3 Combat

- **Camera:** Fixed isometric camera, angle and zoom tuned for phone screens.
- **Controls:** Tap or click to move and attack. Skill bar at the bottom of the
  screen with a small number of slots. Same scheme on phone and desktop.
- **Scale:** Designed for packs. Up to two hundred monsters may be alive and
  visible at once. Monsters are threatening in numbers, not individually.
- **Difficulty:** Normal, Nightmare and Hell, unlocked in sequence. Details TBD.

### 4.4 Levels and world structure

- **Procedural generation every run,** like Diablo 2. Layout rules are
  hand-designed, the arrangement is random. Towns and boss arenas are fixed.
- Acts, each with a town, several outdoor and dungeon areas, and an act boss.
  Number of acts TBD; the vertical slice has one act with one dungeon.

### 4.5 Items

- **Rarity tiers:** Common, Magic, Rare, Mythic, Set (from transmutation only)
  and Divine, dropping 55/25/14/5/0/1 out of 100.
- **Random affixes:** one for magic, two for rare, three for mythic and set,
  four for divine, from a pool of eight, scaled by rarity and item level.
- **Slots:** weapon, shield, helmet, chest, gloves, boots, belt, amulet, two
  rings, totem, relic, charm. An 18x14 bag and a three-page stash of 12x12.
- **Crafting:** Forge of Heaven, Blood Fountain and Arcana Oracle in town.
- **Visible gear:** weapons and shields change the hero's appearance; other
  slots later.

### 4.6 Death

- Respawn in town with no penalty. Friendly for short phone sessions.
- Hardcore mode may be revisited later.

### 4.7 Saves

- Saved in the browser on the device. Autosave on town visits and level exits.
- Export and import a save code to move between devices.
- Cloud saves arrive with co-op, when a server exists anyway.

### 4.8 Multiplayer

- Single-player first. Simulation is kept separate from rendering so that
  server-authoritative co-op can be added later without a rewrite.

## 5. Art and audio direction

- **Rule:** All art is generated by code at runtime. No image, model, texture,
  font or audio files are committed. See the technical design for what this
  means in practice.
- **Style:** Isometric pixel art (decided 2026-09-27 after the art lab review;
  the earlier flat-shaded 3D look was dropped as "fake 3D"). A 640x360 frame
  scaled up by a whole number so every pixel is a crisp square, 2:1 diamond
  tiles of 32x16 pixels, a 22-pixel hero, dark outlines on every sprite, the
  Grim palette (cold stone, deep shadow, warm fire). Characters are drawn from
  three sides (side, front, back) so equipment can swap in and out.
- **Lighting:** Torches, stations and spells are point lights on the frame;
  everything else sinks into darkness in a few dithered bands, the classic
  look. Colour grading stays with cold greys and warm torch orange.
- **Readability:** Monsters must be distinguishable by silhouette and colour at
  phone size. Player, allies, enemies and loot each have a reserved colour
  language.
- **Audio:** Synthesised with the Web Audio API. Percussive hits, low drones,
  crackling spells. Music is generative and sparse.
